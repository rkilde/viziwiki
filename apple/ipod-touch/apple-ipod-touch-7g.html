Three years. The end of the iPod. visual: timeline events: - month: May day: '28' year: '2019' tag: Launch title: iPod touch 7G released. preview: Apple A10 Fusion. 2 GB RAM. New 256 GB top tier. Quiet press-release launch, $199 to start. body:

Apple released the 7th generation iPod touch through its online store on May 28, 2019 — the first major update in nearly four years. It carried the 64-bit Apple A10 Fusion processor (the iPhone 7's chip, underclocked to 1.64 GHz), the M10 motion coprocessor, 2 GB of RAM, and the same 4-inch Retina display, 8 MP rear camera, 1.2 MP front camera, and aluminium body as the 6G.

It launched in space gray, silver, gold, pink, blue, and (PRODUCT)RED — the same six colours as the previous generation — in 32, 128, and 256 GB tiers at $199, $299, and $399. The 256 GB tier was new, the largest capacity any iPod had ever offered. The 16 GB and 64 GB tiers from the 6G era were retired.

- month: May day: '28' year: '2019' tag: Software title: Ships with iOS 12.3, adds Group FaceTime and AR. preview: First iPod to support Group FaceTime and ARKit. iOS 12.3 also ships the redesigned Apple Music app. body:

The 7G shipped with iOS 12.3, released the same day. That version introduced the redesigned Apple Music app with a refreshed Browse tab, and the device was Apple's first iPod to support Group FaceTime (multi-party video calls) and ARKit (augmented reality experiences). Both capabilities had launched on iPhone in 2018 (iOS 12) but required at minimum an A9 chip — too new for the A8-based 6G.

- month: Sep day: '19' year: '2019' tag: Software title: iOS 13 — first iPod ever to run it. preview: iOS 13 dropped every A9 and earlier device. The 7G was the only iPod to make the cut. body:

iOS 13 shipped in September 2019, and the 7G was the only iPod ever to receive it. The release dropped all A8 hardware (cutting off the 6G) and required A9 or newer; the 7G's A10 cleared the bar comfortably. iOS 13 brought Dark Mode, Sign in with Apple, and a redesigned Reminders app, and the 7G handled all of it without compromise.

- month: Sep day: '19' year: '2019' tag: Software title: Apple Arcade launches. preview: Apple's $4.99/month gaming subscription goes live. The 7G is positioned as the cheapest device that can run it. body: '

Apple Arcade — the $4.99/month gaming service Apple had previewed at the 7G''s launch in May — went live on September 19, 2019. Over 100 exclusive games, no ads, no in-app purchases, offline play. At $199, the 7G was by a wide margin the most affordable iOS device that could meaningfully run the Arcade catalogue, and Apple marketed it that way. For many families, the 7G became the de facto Arcade machine: cheap enough to buy as a second device, powerful enough to actually play the games.

' - month: Sep year: '2020' tag: Software title: iOS 14 — full support. preview: Home screen widgets, App Library, picture-in-picture. The 7G handled it all. body:

iOS 14 (September 2020) brought widgets to the iOS home screen for the first time, plus the App Library, picture-in-picture, and significant Messages and Maps updates. The 7G ran it without compromise — by now the only iPod still supported by current iOS.

- month: Sep year: '2021' tag: Software title: iOS 15 — the last major version. preview: Focus modes, SharePlay, Live Text. The final iOS version any iPod will ever run. body:

iOS 15 (September 2021) shipped to the 7G as the last major version it — or any iPod — would ever receive. Features included Focus modes, SharePlay over FaceTime, Live Text in the camera, and a redesigned Safari. The 7G was at this point a four-year-old device but still benchmarked respectably for its class and ran iOS 15 smoothly on its 2 GB of RAM.

- month: May day: '10' year: '2022' tag: Hardware title: Discontinued — end of the iPod line. preview: Apple announces the 7G will be sold while supplies last. The 21-year iPod product line ends. body: '

On May 10, 2022, Apple published a press release titled "The music lives on" announcing the iPod touch would be sold only while supplies last. There was no successor. The iPod line — which had begun on October 23, 2001 with the original click-wheel model and run for 21 years through Classic, mini, nano, shuffle, and seven generations of touch — was ending. Apple framed it as a victory: the iPod''s role had been fully absorbed into iPhone, Apple Watch, HomePod, iPad, Mac, and Apple Music.

Greg Joswiak, Apple''s senior vice president of Worldwide Marketing: "Music has always been part of our core at Apple, and bringing it to hundreds of millions of users in the way iPod did impacted more than just the music industry — it also redefined how music is discovered, listened to, and shared."

' - month: May day: '12' year: '2022' tag: Hardware title: 256 GB models sell out within 48 hours. preview: A day after the discontinuation announcement, every 256 GB colour sells out on Apple's US store. body:

Within roughly two days of the discontinuation announcement, the 256 GB iPod touch had sold out in every colour on the US Apple Store. Other capacities followed within the week. Resale prices on eBay briefly spiked above the original $399 MSRP as buyers competed for the last new-in-box units. The 7G — and the iPod line — was effectively gone from Apple's first-party inventory within days, not the weeks or months 'while supplies last' usually implies.

- month: Sep year: '2022' tag: Software title: iOS 16 — excluded. preview: iOS 16 drops the A10 generation. iPhone 7, 7 Plus, and the 7G fall off the list together. body:

iOS 16 (September 2022) raised the minimum supported chip to A11. The iPhone 7, iPhone 7 Plus, and 7G — all A10 Fusion devices — fell off the support list in the same release. The 7G stayed on iOS 15 as its final major version, definitively ending iPod's run on current iOS.

- month: Mar day: '11' year: '2026' tag: Software title: iOS 15.8.7 — latest security update. preview: Nearly four years after discontinuation, the 7G is still receiving security patches from Apple. body:

iOS 15.8.7, released March 11, 2026, was the most recent iOS update available to the 7G at time of writing — nearly four years after Apple discontinued the iPod line and over six years after the device launched. The release was a maintenance update rather than a feature one, but it kept iMessage, FaceTime, App Store, and device activation working. For an A10-based device with no successor and no commercial reason to maintain support, the continued security patches were a quietly generous extension of an already-long support tail.
