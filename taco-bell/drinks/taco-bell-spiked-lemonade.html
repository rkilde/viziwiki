Overview

Background

Mtn Dew Spiked was a lemonade-flavored Mountain Dew variant introduced in early 2017 as a replacement for Sangrita Blast on Taco Bell’s dedicated Mountain Dew fountain valve. The drink was marketed as being “spiked” with prickly pear cactus juice, though it contained no alcohol.

PepsiCo simultaneously released retail 16-ounce cans in two flavors: Lemonade and Raspberry Lemonade. The drink was promoted during Comedy Central’s Colossal Clusterfest in 2017.

A Freeze variant was available at Taco Bell from early 2017 through September 2017, when it was replaced by the Strawberry Poppin’ Candy Freeze. Retail cans were discontinued in 2018. The Taco Bell fountain version was phased out between December 2018 and March 2019 to make room for the introduction of Baja Blast Zero Sugar.

In Canada, the product was sold under the name “Mtn Dew Extra (Lemonade)” to avoid confusion about the “Spiked” name’s implied alcohol content.

Flavor profile

Flavor characteristics

Spiked Lemonade was a citrus-forward, lighter-bodied drink compared to its predecessor Sangrita Blast. The prickly pear cactus juice addition was a departure from typical Mountain Dew formulations. Consumer reviews described it as having a standard lemonade base with a slight botanical undertone. Some reviewers noted an unusual aftertaste attributed to the prickly pear component.

Distribution

Channel availability

Spiked Lemonade launched across three channels nearly simultaneously in early 2017: Taco Bell fountain, retail cans, and Freeze. The Freeze was removed first (September 2017), retail cans followed (2018), and the fountain version was the last to go (March 2019).

Taco Bell

Relationship with Taco Bell

Spiked Lemonade replaced Sangrita Blast as the second dedicated Mountain Dew flavor on Taco Bell fountains. Unlike Sangrita Blast, which was developed specifically as a Taco Bell exclusive, Spiked Lemonade was simultaneously available in retail, making it less of a franchise-exclusive product.

The drink occupied the Taco Bell fountain valve for approximately two years—half the tenure of its predecessor. When it was removed, Baja Blast Zero Sugar took the valve, becoming the third tenant in six years. The naming controversy (see Overview) may have contributed to underperformance at the franchise level.

Legacy

Reception and afterlife

Spiked Lemonade did not generate the same level of nostalgia as Sangrita Blast after its discontinuation. There are no known petitions for its return. Online discussions about the product focus primarily on the naming controversy and its short lifespan relative to Sangrita Blast.

The drink’s most lasting significance may be structural: it demonstrated that the second Mountain Dew valve at Taco Bell is an inherently volatile position, cycling through tenants at an accelerating pace.
