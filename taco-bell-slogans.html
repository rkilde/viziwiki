Overview

From no tagline to Live Más.

Taco Bell was founded by Glen Bell in Downey, California in 1962 and operated for fifteen years without a national advertising tagline, growing to more than a thousand locations on the strength of its menu and price point alone. That changed in 1977 when PepsiCo — which had acquired the chain the previous year — brought the marketing infrastructure required to push a coordinated national voice. The Fresh Food Place became the first tagline with genuine national reach.

What followed was a forty-year search for the right register. The 1980s moved quickly through four slogans in roughly a decade, each attempting to land on a differentiator — freshness, customisation, friendliness — before the chain discovered in 1988 that its most powerful advertising asset was atmosphere rather than product. Make a Run for the Border ran for six years and embedded itself in American vernacular, particularly among the late-night college audience.

The 1990s produced Taco Bell's most studied campaign: Yo Quiero Taco Bell and Gidget the Chihuahua. Brand awareness hit record highs while same-store sales declined — a real-world demonstration that recognition and purchase intent are distinct quantities. The campaign was retired in 2001 and became the subject of a $42 million lawsuit over creative ownership.

Think Outside the Bun then ran for eleven years, the longest primary slogan in the chain's history. In 2012, at the 50th anniversary mark, the chain replaced it with two words: Live Más. Launched alongside Doritos Locos Tacos — the best-selling product launch in fast-food history — the tagline repositioned Taco Bell from a food destination to a lifestyle brand. It is still active today.
