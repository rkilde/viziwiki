Overview

An American chain that invented its own genre.

Taco Bell is an American fast-food chain founded in 1962 by Glen Bell in Downey, California. Built on a single observation — that long lines at the Mexican restaurant across the street meant Americans wanted tacos faster — it became the country's largest Mexican-inspired quick-service chain and the only one to operate at national scale.

More than a restaurant, Taco Bell functions as a cultural laboratory: a permanent menu of roughly 30 items rotated against a relentless churn of limited-time releases, novelty crossovers (Doritos, Mountain Dew, Cheetos), and viral comebacks (Mexican Pizza, Volcano Menu). The chain pioneered the fourthmeal — a marketing-coined late-night occasion — and the concept of fast food as fan culture, complete with petitions, merch drops, and a musical.

Today the company operates over 7,200 restaurants across 31 countries under Yum! Brands, alongside KFC and Pizza Hut. Its reach extends well beyond the menu: into film cameos, sauce-packet fortune cookies, hotel residencies, a clothing line, and the only fast-food brand with a nail polish collection.

Footprint

A purple sign in 31 countries.

Taco Bell operates more than 7,200 restaurants worldwide, the vast majority in the United States. International expansion has been famously bumpy — Mexico’s launch lasted just two years — but the chain has found steady growth across the UK, Spain, India, and the Middle East.

Marketing · Slogans

From no tagline to Live Más.

Taco Bell has used eleven distinct taglines since 1962 — from no slogan at all through regional experiments, era-defining campaigns, and a landmark federal lawsuit, to the two-word brand identity still in use today. Each slogan marks a strategic shift in how the chain positioned itself to American consumers.

"Make a Run for the Border."
Slogan 05 · 1988 – 1994
The first era-defining line. Entered everyday speech and turned Taco Bell into a late-night destination rather than just a food stop.
"Yo Quiero Taco Bell."
Slogan 07 · 1997 – 2001
Peak brand awareness, declining sales. The dog was famous. The food wasn't ordered. A textbook case in the gap between recognition and purchase intent.
"Think Outside the Bun."
Slogan 08 · 2001 – 2012 · Longest run
Eleven years — the longest primary slogan in Taco Bell history. A direct competitive challenge to the burger, with the Crunchwrap as its product proof point.
"Live Más."
Slogan 09 · 2012 – present · Active
Launched alongside Doritos Locos Tacos in 2012. Repositioned Taco Bell from a fast-food chain to a lifestyle brand. Still active after fourteen years.

Legal · Wrench LLC v. Taco Bell Corp.
The $42M lawsuit behind the Chihuahua campaign.

In 1995, Thomas Rinks and Joseph Shields — two designers based in Grand Rapids, Michigan — created Psycho Chihuahua, a feisty cartoon dog they licensed through their company Wrench LLC. A year later, Taco Bell executives approached them at a New York trade show and expressed interest in the concept for advertising. During those discussions, a Taco Bell creative manager acknowledged that if the concept was used, Wrench would need to be compensated. No contract was signed.

In 1997, Taco Bell launched the Chihuahua campaign through agency TBWA Chiat/Day — and paid Wrench nothing. Wrench sued in 1998. After the district court dismissed all claims as preempted by copyright law, the Sixth Circuit reversed in a landmark 2001 ruling, holding that implied-in-fact contract claims survive federal preemption. A federal jury returned a verdict for Wrench in June 2003, awarding $30,174,031 — growing to over $42 million with interest.

Chihuahua copyright suit

Wrench Inc. won $42M; jury found agency lifted the dog concept from a 1996 pitch.

2011
'Seasoned beef' class action

Suit alleged beef was 35% meat. Dropped after Taco Bell published the recipe (88% beef).

E. coli outbreak

71 cases across 5 states traced to lettuce. Locations temporarily closed; supply chain overhauled.

Mexico exit

Second attempt at Mexican market closed within two years — 'fries and ketchup' positioning failed.
