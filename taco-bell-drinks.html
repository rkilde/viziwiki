Overview

The only fast-food chain whose drink is its own brand.

Taco Bell's beverage program is anchored by a single product nobody else can sell: Mountain Dew Baja Blast. Co-developed by PepsiCo and Taco Bell flavor R&D in 2003 and launched in February 2004, it is the only Mountain Dew variant manufactured exclusively for a single restaurant chain — and it transformed Taco Bell from a Pepsi licensee into a beverage destination in its own right.

The full lineup divides into six families: the Baja products (carbonated, freeze, and the growing retail bottle line), the Freeze wall (slushed Mountain Dew variants — currently Baja, Wild Cherry, and a rotating third valve), fountain Pepsi standards, the Brisk × Taco Bell juice and tea pairings, the breakfast coffee program introduced in 2014, and a small slate of bottled cooler drinks. The fountain has been all-Pepsi since the 1997 Tricon spinoff that created what is now Yum! Brands.

Taken together, drinks generate roughly 18% of an average store's revenue and a disproportionate share of its margin — Baja Blast alone has been credited with carrying entire product launches (notably the Crunchwrap and Mexican Pizza relaunches), and the freezer-aisle bottle release has become its own annual cultural event.

The Baja Blast lineage

2003 → today

Baja Blast is the only soda PepsiCo manufactures for a single chain. Below: every meaningful event in its 20-year run, from the internal R&D codename to the 2024 sports tie-ins.

  1. 2003 R&D PepsiCo and Taco Bell R&D begin work on a custom Mtn Dew flavor — internal codename 'Project Caribbean.'
  2. Feb 2004 Launch Baja Blast launches at U.S. Taco Bell fountains. Marketed as 'tropical lime' and exclusive forever.
  3. 2010 Spinoff Mtn Dew Sangrita Blast (cherry-citrus) introduced; quietly retired in 2012.
  4. 2014 + Freeze Baja Blast Freeze debuts. Same year, the freezer wall expands across most stores.
  5. 2014 Bottle Limited bottled run hits grocery shelves for summer. Sells out in weeks; spawns 'Baja Blast Day' (July 8).
  6. 2018 Test Pineapple-flavor Baja Blast Tropical Lime Splash tested in Pacific Northwest; never expands.
  7. 2020 Permanent Bottled Baja Blast becomes a permanent grocery SKU sold by PepsiCo.
  8. 2024 Crossover Baja Blast Gel Energy (a hydration gel) launches in partnership with Mtn Dew × Live Más.

Did you know
Drink fact№ 01

Baja Blast is the only soda PepsiCo manufactures exclusively for a single restaurant chain.

Drink fact№ 02

The Freeze machines in Taco Bell are made by Taylor Company — the same supplier behind McDonald's notorious McFlurry machines.

Drink fact№ 03

The 'Baja' name is a marketing reference to Baja California, Mexico — though no part of the recipe is Mexican.

Drink fact№ 04

Bottled Baja Blast was originally a 12-week LTO in 2014; demand caused PepsiCo to extend it three times before going permanent.

Drink fact№ 05

Cinnabon Delights Coffee uses a syrup co-developed with Cinnabon's flavor lab in Atlanta — not a generic cinnamon dolce.

Drink fact№ 06

A 30 oz Baja Blast Freeze contains roughly 110 g of sugar — more than two standard Snickers bars.

Drink fact№ 07

Taco Bell's only nationally rolled-back beverage decision is the 2020 Lipton Brisk Lemon → Raspberry swap, which still gets petitions.
